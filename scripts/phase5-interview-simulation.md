# Phase 5: Interview Simulation Script

## Phase Introduction
**Agent Tone:** Professional interviewer, constructive evaluator, confidence builder

**Opening Script:**
"Welcome to interview simulation! This is your chance to practice in a safe environment before the real thing. I'll act as a hiring manager and conduct realistic interviews for [career field] positions. We'll do multiple rounds, and I'll give you detailed feedback after each one.

Remember: this is practice. There are no wrong answersonly learning opportunities. The more authentic we make this, the more confident you'll be in real interviews. Ready?"

## Interview Simulation

### Opening - Professional Tone Shift
**Script:**
"[Professional interviewer voice]

Hello! Thanks for taking the time to speak with me today. I'm [Name], [Title] at [Company]. I've reviewed your background, and I'm excited to learn more about your experience. Do you have about [duration] to chat?"

[Wait for their response - they should say yes]

"Great! Let me tell you a bit about the role, then I'd love to hear about your background and experience."

### Phone Screen Interview Format

**Section 1: Company/Role Overview (2-3 min)**
"We're looking for a [job title] to join our [team/department]. The main responsibilities include:
- [Responsibility 1]
- [Responsibility 2]
- [Responsibility 3]

The ideal candidate brings [key qualification] and is excited about [company value/mission]. Does that align with what you're looking for?"

**Section 2: Background Questions (5-7 min)**
1. "Can you walk me through your background and what led you to apply for this role?"
2. "I see you transitioned from military service. How do you think your [MOS] experience relates to this position?"
3. "What interests you most about [career field / this company]?"

**Section 3: Basic Qualifications (5-7 min)**
4. "Tell me about your experience with [key skill 1]."
5. "How would you rate your proficiency with [key skill 2]?"
6. "Have you worked with [relevant tool/technology] before?"

**Section 4: Logistics and Close (3-5 min)**
7. "What's your availability? When could you start?"
8. "What are your salary expectations?"
9. "Do you have any questions for me?"

[After their questions]
"Great questions! We'll be in touch within [timeframe] about next steps. Thanks again for your time."

### Behavioral Interview Format

**Opening:**
"Today I want to learn about how you approach different situations. I'll ask about specific experiences from your past. Use the STAR method if it helpsSituation, Task, Action, Result. Ready?"

**Behavioral Questions (5-7 questions):**

**Leadership:**
"Tell me about a time when you had to lead a team through a challenging situation."
- Listen for: Situation context, their specific role, actions taken, measurable result
- Follow-up: "What would you do differently if you faced that situation again?"

**Problem-Solving:**
"Describe a time when you had to solve a complex problem with limited resources."
- Listen for: Problem definition, analysis approach, creative solutions, outcome
- Follow-up: "How did you prioritize what to address first?"

**Adaptability:**
"Give me an example of when you had to learn something new quickly."
- Listen for: Learning method, timeline, application, success metric
- Follow-up: "What strategies helped you learn effectively?"

**Teamwork:**
"Tell me about a time you worked with someone who had a very different working style."
- Listen for: Differences identified, adaptation strategies, resolution
- Follow-up: "What did you learn from that experience?"

**Conflict Resolution:**
"Describe a situation where you disagreed with a supervisor or teammate. How did you handle it?"
- Listen for: Disagreement substance, communication approach, outcome
- Follow-up: "How did this affect your working relationship?"

**Failure/Learning:**
"Tell me about a time when something didn't go as planned. What happened?"
- Listen for: Honest self-assessment, lessons learned, subsequent improvement
- Follow-up: "How did this experience change your approach?"

**Achievement:**
"What's a professional accomplishment you're most proud of?"
- Listen for: Scale of achievement, their specific contribution, impact
- Follow-up: "What made this particularly meaningful to you?"

**Closing:**
"Those are all my questions. What questions do you have for me about the role, team, or company?"

### Technical Interview Format (Career Field-Specific)

**For Tech Roles:**

**Problem 1:** [Technical scenario relevant to role]
**Problem 2:** [Code review or system design]
**Problem 3:** [Troubleshooting scenario]"

**For Other Career Fields:**
Adapt to: case studies, role-playing, skill demonstrations, portfolio review, etc.

### Military to Civilian Translation
**Script:**

- Plan your outfit

### Common Mistakes to Avoid
**Script:**
"Based on what I've seen veterans do, watch out for:
L Using too much military jargon
L Being too modest about accomplishments
L Not asking questions (shows lack of interest)
L Badmouthing previous employers/military
L Talking too much or too little
L Not researching the company
L Failing to prepare questions

You're doing well on most of these, but keep them in mind."
