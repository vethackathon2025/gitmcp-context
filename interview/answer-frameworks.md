# Answer Frameworks for Curriculum Questions

This file contains interviewer intent, scoring rubrics, and sample strong/weak answer indicators mapped to the `interview/question-banks.md` questions. Use these frameworks to standardize scoring and feedback.

## Exercises — Representative frameworks

Q: Describe an exercise you would create to teach a difficult concept from this curriculum. What are the learning objectives, steps, and expected outcomes?

- Intent: Assess ability to design clear, measurable, and pedagogically-sound activities tied to learning objectives.
- What to look for: explicit objectives, stepwise learner flow, assessment method, alignment to outcomes, feasibility.
- Scoring (0-3):
	- 3 = Clear objectives, step-by-step plan, measurable outcomes, and built-in feedback loops; feasible and accessible.
	- 2 = Good objectives and flow but missing clear measurement or accessibility considerations.
	- 1 = Vague objectives or steps; little sense of assessment.
	- 0 = No coherent plan or objectives.
- Sample strong answer (bullets):
	- Learning objective: learners can X by performing Y with Z accuracy.
	- Steps: brief demo -> guided practice -> independent task -> reflection.
	- Assessment: rubric with 3 criteria; automated check + instructor spot-check.
	- Accessibility: captions, low-bandwidth alternative files.
- Red flags: no measurable outcomes, only lecture-style activity, no feedback mechanism.

Q: How would you adapt an exercise for learners with limited time or lower baseline knowledge?

- Intent: Evaluate ability to prioritize core learning and scaffold for different learners.
- What to look for: triage of core vs nice-to-have, modularization, optional pre-work, reduced-scope tasks.
- Scoring (0-3):
	- 3 = Presents concise core path, optional depth modules, and quick assessment to validate readiness.
	- 2 = Suggests sensible reductions but lacks clear triage criteria.
	- 1 = Generic suggestion ("make it shorter") without concrete approach.
	- 0 = No adaptation strategy.

## Learning Resources — Representative frameworks

Q: How do you choose high-quality learning resources for a module? List criteria and give an example.

- Intent: Assess curation judgment and concrete quality criteria (accuracy, currency, readability, level-appropriateness).
- What to look for: evidence of vetting (author credentials, publication date), alignment to objectives, accessibility, licensing.
- Scoring (0-3):
	- 3 = Clear, multi-factor criteria + a concrete example with justification.
	- 2 = Good criteria but weaker justification or example.
	- 1 = Superficial criteria or no example.
	- 0 = No criteria.

Q: Explain how you would keep resource lists current and what signals you'd use to retire an item.

- Intent: Evaluate operational processes for maintenance and currency.
- What to look for: scheduled reviews, usage metrics, broken-link checks, community feedback, field changes.
- Scoring (0-3):
	- 3 = Concrete schedule/automation + signals & owner for decisions.
	- 2 = Good signals but ad-hoc process.
	- 1 = Vague monitoring.
	- 0 = No plan.

## Module Library — Representative frameworks

Q: Describe the structure you would use for a reusable module (sections, metadata, prerequisites, outcomes).

- Intent: Check for modular thinking and metadata design enabling reuse.
- What to look for: clear learning outcomes, time estimates, prerequisites, success metrics, author and version metadata.
- Scoring (0-3):
	- 3 = Complete structure with metadata examples and rationale.
	- 2 = Good structure but missing some metadata fields.
	- 1 = Fragmented structure, unclear reuse story.
	- 0 = No coherent structure.

Q: Give an approach to versioning and maintaining modules across cohorts.

- Intent: Assess lifecycle planning and practical change management.
- What to look for: semantic versioning or changelog, owners, backward compatibility, migration notes for learners/instructors.
- Scoring (0-3):
	- 3 = Versioning policy + CI checks + owner responsibilities.
	- 2 = Reasonable plan without automation.
	- 1 = Surface-level ideas.
	- 0 = No plan.

## Resources — Representative frameworks

Q: Explain a simple taxonomy you would use to tag and search resources.

- Intent: Assess information-architecture thinking and practical searchability.
- What to look for: orthogonal tags (topic, level, format, duration, license), examples, and search use-cases.
- Scoring (0-3):
	- 3 = Clear tag schema + examples + explanation of how it improves discovery.
	- 2 = Good tag ideas but incomplete.
	- 1 = Weak taxonomy.
	- 0 = None.

Q: What metadata fields are essential for each resource entry and why?

- Intent: Ensure entries are discoverable, assessable, and safe to use.
- Core fields to expect: title, author/source, date, level, format, duration, license, summary, learning objectives, tags, access URL, contact/owner.
- Scoring (0-3):
	- 3 = Lists most fields with rationale and an example of how they'd be used.
	- 2 = Lists some fields with partial rationale.
	- 1 = Minimal fields.
	- 0 = No sensible fields.

---
Notes on usage

- Tailor rubrics to role seniority: junior roles expect clear reasoning and basic structure (2s and 3s require fewer details than for senior hires).
- When in doubt, ask a follow-up: "How would you measure success for this piece?" or "Who owns this and how often would they review it?"
