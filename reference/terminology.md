
# Common Military Terminology (everyday usage)

This reference lists common terms, acronyms, and everyday phrases servicemembers use to describe routine actions, status, and small-unit work. Definitions are short and practical, with a usage note where helpful. The goal is plain-language clarity for civilian readers and for other team members who work with veterans.

Note: language and slang vary by service and unit. This list favors widely used, non-derogatory terms and explains idiomatic phrases that frequently appear in day-to-day reports and conversations.

## Action verbs & common verbs

- Muster — to gather personnel in one place ("Muster at 0700 in the motor pool").
- Stand-up / stand up — to start or establish a regularly scheduled meeting or capability ("Stand-up the watch at 0600").
- Stand-down / stand down — to stop an activity or relax a posture ("Stand down; training is complete").
- Execute — to carry out an order or plan ("We executed the movement plan").
- Execute the plan / execute on — to perform the planned actions.
- Pass/Pass down — to relay information down the chain ("Pass the update down to your squad").
- Sign off — to formally complete or approve ("Sign off on the checklist before departure").
- Check-in — to report presence or status ("Check in when you arrive on site").
- Check-out — to report leaving or transfer of responsibility.
- Conduct (an activity) — to carry out an activity ("Conduct a pre-mission brief").
- Prep/prepare — get ready ("Prep the vehicle for convoy").
- Inspect/inspector — verify condition or compliance ("Inspector will do a kit check").

## Units, roles & people

- Team / Squad — small tactical element (team often 4–12 people depending on service).
- Fireteam — typically a 4-person tactical sub-unit in infantry formations.
- Platoon — element made of multiple squads (often 20–50 personnel).
- Company / Troop / Battery — larger unit made of several platoons.
- Battalion / Regiment — higher echelon unit made of several companies.
- CO (Commanding Officer) — unit commander.
- XO (Executive Officer) — second-in-command.
- NCO (Non-Commissioned Officer) — enlisted leader (sergeants, corporals).
- PO (Petty Officer) — enlisted leadership rank in the Navy.
- Enlisted / Officer — main personnel categories.
- POC (Point of Contact) — the person to contact for a topic.

## Movement, locations & time

- AO (Area of Operations) — the geographic area assigned for operations.
- LZ (Landing Zone) — place where aircraft land; used for helicopters and resupply.
- DZ (Drop Zone) — location where airborne personnel or supplies are dropped.
- POI / POI (Point of Interest or Point of Impact) — location marker.
- Rally point — a pre-agreed location to reassemble after movement.
- TOC (Tactical Operations Center) / HQ — command center.
- Convoy — group movement of vehicles.
- vicked / VICE? (avoid unclear slang) — note: prefer MEDEVAC/CASEVAC below for casualty moves.
- H-hour / H-Hour — time an operation is scheduled to start (H+/- offsets).
- Zulu / Z — Zulu time (UTC) commonly used in ops planning and logs.

## Communications & reporting

- SITREP (Situation Report) — short report on current situation ("Send a SITREP every 4 hours").
- SITREP/BDA (Battle Damage Assessment) — assessment following an engagement.
- SITREP/COMMS — communications status report.
- OPORD (Operation Order) — formal, structured order for an operation (five-paragraph format common in many forces).
- FRAGO (Fragmentary Order) — abbreviated order that modifies an OPORD.
- AAR (After-Action Review) — debrief to capture lessons learned after an event.
- BRIEF / SITREP / Update — short informative messages.
- Call sign — radio identifier for a unit or vehicle.
- Comms check — test to ensure radios/networks are working.

## Logistics & supply

- TOA (Table of Allowances) / TDA — list of authorized equipment and quantities.
- MOS (Military Occupational Specialty) — job code (Army/Marines); other services use rating/AFSC.
- PX / Exchange — retail store on a military base (also "shopette" for smaller stores).
- Chow / Mess — food/meal; chow hall is the dining facility (DFAC).
- Issue / issueable — equipment that is provided by supply.
- Turn-in — return of equipment to supply.
- Pre-positioned stocks / bench stock — inventory kept for reuse.
- Sustainment — logistics activities that support ongoing operations.

## Medical & casualty

- MEDEVAC (Medical Evacuation) — movement of wounded by medical transport (formal medical care en route).
- CASEVAC (Casualty Evacuation) — non-medical transport of casualties (often faster, less equipped than MEDEVAC).
- TACEVAC (Tactical Evacuation) — evacuation under tactical conditions.
- Moulage — training makeup used to simulate injuries during training.
- 9-Line — the standard radio brief used to request MEDEVAC with 9-line casualty format.

## Training, readiness & qualifications

- PT (Physical Training) — exercise sessions.
- Quals / Qualification — weapons or systems qualification ("qual range").
- Certification / currency — being current on a skill (e.g., driver currency, aircraft currency).
- Drill / drill day — practice or scheduled training evolutions.
- Table-top — low-cost planning exercise or simulation done at desks.

## Safety, rules & doctrine

- SOP (Standard Operating Procedure) — written process for recurring tasks.
- ROE (Rules of Engagement) — legal/policy limits on use of force.
- Risk assessment / risk acceptance — evaluating hazards and deciding mitigation.

## Common acronyms & short forms (quick reference)

- ETA — Estimated Time of Arrival.
- ETD — Estimated Time of Departure.
- POC — Point of Contact.
- SOP — Standard Operating Procedure.
- CO / XO — Commanding Officer / Executive Officer.
- NCO — Non-Commissioned Officer.
- LZ / DZ — Landing Zone / Drop Zone.
- TOC — Tactical Operations Center.
- SITREP — Situation Report.
- AAR — After-Action Review.
- OPORD / FRAGO — Operation Order / Fragmentary Order.
- MEDEVAC / CASEVAC — Medical / Casualty Evacuation.

## Everyday slang & idioms (what they mean in practice)

- "Hurry up and wait" — common experience where personnel rush to prepare then wait for orders.
- "Stand by" — be ready; wait for further instruction.
- "On my six" — I'm watching your rear ("I've got your six").
- "Boots on the ground" — forces physically present in an area.
- "Green light / red light" — permission to proceed / stop.
- "Squared away" — well organized, ready, or corrected ("Are you squared away?").
- "Joe" / "G.I. Joe" (informal) — a generic servicemember; usage varies.
- "Chit" — small paper form for administrative requests or privileges (e.g., leave chit).

## Usage tips for civilian readers and interviewers

- Favor the long form when precision matters (e.g., "MEDEVAC" not just "evac").
- When interviewing or onboarding, ask for a brief example: "When you said 'stand down', what happened next?" — context clarifies intent.
- Avoid assuming slang is universal across services; always verify meaning with the speaker if something's unclear.
